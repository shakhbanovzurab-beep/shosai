# connectors

The registry of the services a person or a workspace connected, and their grants, on Shosai's own OAuth core: a person connects Gmail, a calendar, a drive or a CRM once, and every part of Shosai that needs it uses that one connection; Shosai keeps the grant alive, tells the person once when only they can fix it, and forgets it when they disconnect or leave.

- Plan: [docs/plan/slices/connectors.md](../../../../../docs/plan/slices/connectors.md). Decisions: CN-D1…CN-D12 in the plan and [ADR 0075](../../../../../docs/adr/0075-connections-on-own-oauth-core-without-nango.md) (no Nango).
- Public surface (`contracts/`): `CONNECTIONS` — a person's or a workspace's connections and their state, never a token; `PROVIDER_ACCESS` — an authorized `ProviderSession` on one connection (tasks' Jira, connectors' own adapters); `INSTALLATIONS` — chats installs Slack into a workspace; `CONNECTOR_USES` — a consumer declares the apps it uses, the catalogue offers only those (CN-D4). Events `connectors.connection.connected`, `.broken` (once per transition into needing the person) and `.revoked`, one per connection.

## Domain (step 1)

- **Catalogue** (`domain/catalogue.py`, plan §3.2): the 21 apps of the owner's list, each with its provider, the OAuth application its new grants come from (`client_key`; Gmail has a Google project of its own while it waits for CASA, CN-D5), its capability, the cabinet's category (the six plus `calendar`), how it authenticates (OAuth; an app password for Yandex Calendar, CalDAV and Mail.ru's fallback; an API key for Affinity; a Catch Hook URL for Zapier) and its scopes. Drive asks only for `drive.file`; Gmail for `gmail.modify` alone; only Slack installs into a workspace, with its bot's scopes.
- **Descriptor** (`domain/descriptors.py`): a provider's endpoints (HTTPS only), how the client authenticates at the token endpoint, PKCE, the scope separator, extra authorize parameters (never the core's own), rotating refresh tokens, the idle expiry, the revocation or none, the union of scopes for providers with one token per account, quotas and concurrency, timeouts within a minute. Each provider's descriptor comes with its step.
- **States** (`domain/states.py`): a grant is `active`, `needs_reconnect` with a problem or `revoking`; a connection shows its grant's problem first, then its own (`scope_missing`); `needs_reconnect` exactly when there is a problem; `broken` is news only on the way from working to waiting.
- **Scopes** (`domain/scopes.py`): a flow asks for the app's scopes, and for the union with the account's grant where the provider keeps one token per account; a grant covers an app when nothing is missing — Google lets a person untick a box.
- **Flows** (`domain/flows.py`): PKCE S256 (RFC 7636's vector), a 32-byte state and a 32-byte browser binder kept as SHA-256 and compared in constant time, return targets — the popup's `/oauth/callback`, a cabinet path under `/app` (never absolute, never `//` or `..`), chats with its `installation_id` — and the relative `Location` with `status` and an error code.
- **Refresh** (`domain/refresh.py`): refresh two minutes before expiry; a token endpoint's answer is refreshed, a refused grant, a misconfigured client (an alert, no grant broken) or transient; a keep-alive two weeks before an idle expiry, spread by a caller-drawn value, never sooner than 12 hours.

## Tables (step 2)

- `connector_grants`, `connector_grant_secrets`, `connections` in each home (revision `81bcb965a31c`): a personal row (`org_id` empty) is its person's alone — company isolation plus a restrictive policy; a workspace's installation follows the company policy. One app per person; connections sit on a grant of the same person; secrets cascade with their grant.
- Account data (`application/account_data.py`): export without a ciphertext, a transfer with ciphertexts unchanged, erasure of personal rows only — a workspace's installation stays with the workspace.

## OAuth core (step 3)

- **Start** (`application/authorize.py`, `POST /v1/connectors/{app}/authorize {return: popup | page, return_path?}`): the app must be offered (`application/offers.py`: a consumer's `CONNECTOR_USES` and a configured OAuth application) and its owner allowed; the scopes are the app's, with the union of the owner's grant where the provider keeps one token per account; the flow lives 10 minutes in Valkey under the SHA-256 of its state (`infrastructure/oauth/flow_store.py`), with the PKCE verifier and the browser binder's digest; the response sets `shosai_connect` (httpOnly, secure, `SameSite=Lax`, path `/v1/connectors/oauth`). Unknown app 404 `unknown_app`, not offered 409 `not_offered`, a return path outside `/app` 422 `return_path_invalid`, Valkey or the key ring missing 503 `connect_unavailable`.
- **Callback** (`application/callback.py`, `GET /v1/connectors/oauth/{provider}/callback`): the flow is taken once with `GETDEL` and must come back to the browser that started it; `access_denied` is `denied`, a provider's own error maps through its adapter (`admin_consent_required`); the code is exchanged outside any transaction (`infrastructure/oauth/tokens.py`: Basic, form or JSON client authentication, the verifier), the granted scopes must cover the app's (`scope_missing`), the adapter reads the account; one transaction upserts the account's grant (scopes accumulate, a new token replaces the old one under core's Fernet ring), puts the app on it and writes `connectors.connection.connected` — an app reconnected with another account ends its old connection (`revoked`, `replaced`) and leaves an orphaned grant `revoking`. Every way out is a 303 to a relative cabinet path with a code.
- **Installations** (`application/installations.py`, `INSTALLATIONS`): chats starts one for a workspace; the callback checks again that the person is the workspace's owner or admin (`MEMBERSHIP_READER`) and writes the audit row (`settings_changed`, target `integration`, `<app>:installed`); removal writes `<app>:removed` and the event.
- **Draft paths**: `/v1/integrations/oauth/{google,microsoft,slack,notion,attio,hubspot}/callback` and `/v1/jira/oauth/callback` answer an expired authorization.
- **Providers** (`infrastructure/providers/`): an adapter gives its descriptor, reads its tokens, says who the account is and maps its callback errors; `configured.py` lists the configured applications — none until the provider steps.

## Grant lifecycle (step 4)

- **Tokens** (`application/grant_tokens.py`): a grant's secrets are read once; a token expiring within two minutes, or one the provider answered 401 to, is refreshed by the lease's holder — a conditional update on `refresh_generation`, 30 seconds — outside any transaction, and written on the same generation (three attempts inside the lease; a lost rotated token is counted). A loser polls for three seconds, then `provider_busy`. `invalid_grant` breaks the grant (`refresh_refused`) with `connectors.connection.broken` for each app that newly shows it (`application/health.py`); `invalid_client` counts `shosai_connectors_client_misconfigured_total` and breaks nothing; anything else is transient and counted on the grant.
- **Due rows** (`application/lifecycle_sweeps.py`, `channels/tasks.py`): the sweeps read across a home as the sweep role (keep-alive every 5 minutes, revocations every 30 seconds); each due grant becomes a task in its owner's scope that claims it with a conditional update, so the sweep role never writes. The keep-alive refreshes an idle grant before its provider forgets it; a failure waits an hour.
- **Disconnect** (`application/disconnect.py`): the app's connection and `revoked` in one transaction; the grant goes `revoking` (with `revoke_at`, revision `4b32f8e17344`) only when its last app is gone. **Revocation** (`application/revocation.py`): claimed by moving `revoke_at` a lease forward, the adapter's `revoke` (`infrastructure/oauth/revocation.py` for RFC 7009), then the grant and its secrets go; a refusal waits 2, 4, 8, 16 minutes and the fifth forgets the grant anyway, counted by provider. Erasure revokes each personal grant once, at once, before the rows go.
- **Workspaces** (`channels/subscriptions.py`): `workspaces.workspace.deleted` ends a workspace's installations (`revoked`, `workspace_deleted`) and leaves their grants for revocation, until workspaces' deletion saga brings its handler.
- **Keys** (`application/key_rotation.py`): weekly per home, re-encryption under the active key while `FERNET_KEYS_PREVIOUS` is set, a check otherwise; `shosai_connectors_undecryptable_secrets` per home.
- **Monitors** (`objectives.py`): `ConnectorSecretUndecryptable`, `ConnectorRefreshLost`, `ConnectorClientMisconfigured`, with runbook sections in `docs/runbooks/alerts.md`.

## Provider transport (step 5)

- **`PROVIDER_ACCESS`** (`application/provider_access.py`): `open(scope, connection_id)` reads the connection in the caller's scope (a stranger's is `not_connected`), refuses one that needs its person at once (`needs_reconnect`), and yields an `AuthorizedSession` in the grant owner's scope.
- **The session** (`infrastructure/transport/session.py`): one `ProviderCall` named by its operation (`slice.action`, HTTPS only); the token in `Authorization` (`Bearer`, `OAuth` for Yandex), refreshed when it expires and once more on a 401; a second 401 with a fresh token breaks the grant (`grant_revoked`). A 429, or a 503 with `Retry-After`, stops the account for every process (`provider_busy{retry_after}`); a 5xx or a transport failure counts toward the provider's breaker, a read is tried once more with jitter, a write never; every other answer is the consumer's to read.
- **Guards** (`infrastructure/transport/guards.py`, Valkey): cooldowns per account, a breaker per provider (five failures in 30 s open it for 20 s; a person's 401, 403 or 429 never counts), the descriptor's quotas as GCRA limits, a semaphore where the provider bounds an account's concurrent calls; Valkey silent for 50 ms lets the call through, logged once.
- **Telemetry:** span `connectors.provider.call` with the provider, the operation and the status; `shosai_connectors_provider_requests_total{provider, operation, outcome}` and `shosai_connectors_provider_request_seconds` — never a URL, an id or a token.

## The cabinet's routes (step 6)

- **List** (`application/cabinet_list.py`, `GET /v1/connectors`): the offered apps and the person's own connections in the catalogue's order — an app is listed while it is offered or connected, so it can always be disconnected; each row with its `auth`, `status` (`connected`, `needs_reconnect`, `not_connected`), `problem`, the account and `access_remains_at_provider` (other apps share the grant, or the provider has no revocation).
- **Disconnect** (`DELETE /v1/connectors/{app}`): 204; `not_connected` for an app the person has not connected, whoever else has.
- **A given secret** (`application/credentials.py`, `POST /v1/connectors/{app}/credentials {login?, password · api_key · webhook_url, address?}`): exactly one secret; the app's probe — brought by its provider's step — checks it before it is kept under core's ring (`credentials_rejected` 422), then the grant, the app and the events as for OAuth; an app without a probe is `not_offered`.
- **A scope picker** (`application/resources.py`, `GET /v1/connectors/{app}/resources?kind&parent&q&cursor&limit`): the app's browser lists one kind of place through the person's own provider session; another kind or an app without a browser is `resources_unsupported` 404; `provider_busy` 503 carries `Retry-After`.
- **Limits** per person: authorize 10, credentials 5 and resources 60 a minute (429 with `Retry-After`).
- **`CONNECTIONS`** (`application/registry.py`): a consumer reads views in its own transaction under row-level security — a person's by capability, a workspace's installations, one by id — and whether a personal connection may serve a workspace: only while its person is an active member there (CN-D3).
