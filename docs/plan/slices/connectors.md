# connectors — slice plan

Prompt: [prompts/S-feature-slice.md](../prompts/S-feature-slice.md), launched through [BACKEND-next-step.md](../prompts/BACKEND-next-step.md). Status: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md), row `connectors`. Slice card: [SLICE_MAP.md §4.2.13](../SLICE_MAP.md#13-connectors). Design, normative: [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../design/ARCHITECTURE.md) §4.1, §6, §7, §9 (inv-2, inv-12, inv-14, inv-17), §10, §12, §13, §14 rules 1, 7, 12–13, §15; the [Leo architecture](../../design/leo/ARCHITECTURE.md) §7.4, §8, §9.3, §13.1; [ADR 0075](../../adr/0075-connections-on-own-oauth-core-without-nango.md). Cabinet features: `settings` (Settings › Connectors and the `/oauth/callback` page); consumers that pick from the registry: `knowledge-base` (Sources), `kanban` (Jira), `chats` (Slack installations), `mail`, `calendar`.

`connectors` is the blade that lets Shosai reach the person's other tools. A person connects Gmail, a calendar, a drive or a CRM once, and every part of Shosai that needs it — the calendar, the inbox, knowledge, the Kanban's Jira, Leo — uses that one connection without asking again. Shosai keeps the grant alive by itself, tells the person once when only they can fix something ("reconnect Google"), and forgets the grant completely when they disconnect or leave.

- **Status:** written on 2026-10-28; **approved by the owner on 2026-10-28** ([ADR 0075](../../adr/0075-connections-on-own-oauth-core-without-nango.md)). The owner's brief had already decided three points — no Nango, the list of providers, the order of the work. With the approval: CN-D1–CN-D4 and CN-D6–CN-D12 as proposed, CN-D2 with a revisit point (several connections of one service — a work and a personal account); CN-D5 amended — no money for CASA now: Google Drive by `drive.file` and Google's Picker, Gmail built but kept in Google's test mode behind a flag until CASA has a budget, Outlook, Yandex Mail and Mail.ru open to everyone at once; Yandex Calendar's connect screen explains, step by step, how to make an app password. §5 lists what the owner provides, §6 how M1 carries the draft's grants.

### 1.2 What the cabinet expects

From the cabinet's code, mocks and i18n (read on 2026-10-28):

- **Settings › Connectors** (`features/settings/domain/connectors-contract.ts`, `ui/connectors/`): 12 apps — `gmail`, `outlook`, `google_drive`, `dropbox`, `yandex_disk`, `notion`, `confluence`, `slack`, `github`, `gitlab`, `jira`, `hubspot` — in six categories (`mail`, `files`, `notes`, `communication`, `tasks`, `crm`). A row is `{id, name, category, connected, account}`. Connect opens a popup synchronously; the API returns `authorize_url`; the provider returns to the backend, which redirects the popup with a **relative** `Location` to `/oauth/callback?connector=…&status=connected|denied|error&error=…`; the page posts `{type: "leo:connector-oauth", connector, status, error}` to the opener (origin-checked) and closes; a blocked popup falls back to a same-tab redirect. Disconnect is optimistic, without confirmation. Three states only: not connected, connecting (client), connected — **no "needs reconnect"**, though Kanban (`reconnect_jira`) and Chats (`token_expired`) send people here to reconnect. `error` is shown verbatim (localized text in the mock).
- **Knowledge › Sources** (`features/knowledge-base/`) is a second registry today: its own 12 ids (adding `obsidian` over GitHub, `sharepoint` over Microsoft, `website` without a grant), its own `PUT /connectors/{id}` with scope fields, a full-page redirect back to `/app/knowledge-base?tab=sources`. SM-13 makes it pick a connection of this registry and keep only the scope — knowledge's frontend step.
- **Kanban › Jira** calls tasks only (`GET /v1/boards/jira/available` → `{connected, account, items}`); "Connect Jira first" opens Settings › Connectors, so the grant is the app `jira` there.
- **Chats › Slack** — the workspace installation above, with its own message type `shosai:chats-oauth`.
- **Mail and calendar** have no connect UI or mailbox API yet; the mail account switcher's "Add account" has no handler. **Notes** has no push UI. **Settings › Leo** has the agent's switch `connected_services_search` ("Search connected services, such as Google Drive and Notion").
- Errors: the Settings mock answers `{detail: text}`; the backend answers ADR 0008's envelope with codes, which `lib/api/errors.ts` already reads.

Refresh tokens work only with the OAuth client that received them, and none of the refresh requests takes a redirect URI: **a grant survives a move to Shosai as long as Shosai refreshes it with the same client id and secret**. What can break that: two refreshers at once with rotating tokens (Atlassian, Notion, GitLab, Yandex, GitHub App, Slack with rotation) — and self-hosted Nango refreshes every connection at least once a day and on every credentials read.

### 1.5 Design point

At 10 000 DAU (100 000 by calculation, §3 of the architecture), with about four connections per active person: ≈ 40 000 connections (400 000) on ≈ 25 000 grants. Access tokens mostly live an hour, so a grant in constant use refreshes about once an hour — at most ≈ 7 refreshes per second at 25 000 grants (70 at the calculated point), far fewer in practice since refresh is lazy. Connects and disconnects are ≪ 1 per second. What matters is the consumers' provider calls — calendar sync, knowledge sync, mail reads, Leo's searches — bounded by each provider's quotas, not by Shosai. The registry read (Settings, a consumer's "which connection") is one indexed query over ≤ 30 rows of one person. The one shared quota that a whole customer base can exhaust is Atlassian's 65 000 points an hour per app (§8).

**Settled without a decision** (derived from the documents; the owner may overrule):

- **Errors:** ADR 0008's envelope with codes; the cabinet words them in 16 languages. `/oauth/callback` receives `error` as a code, as chats already does: `authorization_expired`, `denied`, `scope_missing`, `admin_consent_required` (Microsoft work tenants: the page explains that the organisation's admin must approve Shosai and gives the admin-consent link), `provider_unavailable`, `not_offered`, `credentials_rejected`, `imap_disabled` (Yandex: the page says where to enable it), `provider_subscription_required` (Mail.ru).
- **Disconnect** asks for confirmation in the cabinet (it stops every consumer of the app); where the provider has no revocation, or other apps keep the grant, the confirmation and the row say where Shosai's access remains.
- **No native route**; every connectors route is `web`, except the provider callbacks (`external`). The draft's callback paths (`/v1/integrations/oauth/{google,microsoft,slack,notion,attio,hubspot}/callback`, `/nango/oauth/callback`) answer a flow started on the draft with `/oauth/callback?status=error&error=authorization_expired`; `/v1/jira/oauth/callback` stays the Atlassian redirect URI while the owner keeps it registered.
- **Retries:** `POST …/authorize` creates a short-lived flow and needs no `Idempotency-Key`; `DELETE` is idempotent; `POST …/credentials` probes and stores once, a repeat with the same secret is a no-op.
- **Limits:** authorize 10 a minute per person; credentials 5 a minute per person (no brute force through Shosai's servers); resources 60 a minute per person.
- **Time:** moments in UTC; nothing here is a calendar day.
- **Events** carry ids, the app and a problem or reason code — never an email, a token or a provider account id.
- **Model-facing text** (tool names, descriptions, results, labels) is English and registered in the model-facing test.
- **GitHub and GitLab** stay in the cabinet's `tasks` category.
- **Yandex tokens** are requested with a `device_id` per grant, so a disconnect can revoke them; Slack's token rotation is turned on for the Shosai app (step 20) and the draft's long-lived Slack tokens are exchanged with `oauth.v2.exchange` at their first use.

### 3.4 Connecting

1. The cabinet calls `POST /v1/connectors/{app}/authorize {return: popup | page, return_path?}` with the person's bearer. The server refuses an app not offered (`not_offered`); makes a 32-byte state nonce, a PKCE verifier and a 32-byte browser binder; and keeps the flow in Valkey for 10 minutes under `SHA-256(nonce)`: `{user_id, home, owner, app, client_key, verifier, SHA-256(binder), return, scopes}`. The scopes are the app's; for a provider that holds one token per account (Yandex) or needs it (Atlassian), they are the union with the person's existing grant at that provider, and its account is passed as a login hint. The response sets `shosai_connect=<binder>` (`HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/v1/connectors/oauth`, 10 minutes) and returns `{authorize_url, expires_at}`. Chats' admin route does the same through `INSTALLATIONS.begin` and the contracts' cookie helper.
2. The popup shows the provider's consent; the provider redirects to `GET /v1/connectors/oauth/{provider}/callback?code&state` (or with `error`).
3. The callback takes the flow with `GETDEL` (a replay finds nothing) and compares the binder cookie's hash (another browser → `authorization_expired`). It exchanges the code with the verifier outside any transaction (10 s), checks that the granted scopes cover the app's (Google lets a person untick a box → `scope_missing`), and reads the provider account (id and label). In one transaction it upserts the grant of that provider account — new tokens and the union of scopes — attaches the app's connection to it (moving the app from another account's grant if it was there), and writes `connectors.connected`. A workspace installation also writes its audit row.
4. The callback clears the cookie and answers 303 with a relative `Location`: `/oauth/callback?connector={app}&status=connected` (or `denied`, or `error&error=<code>`); `flow=chats&installation_id=<connection id>` for chats; the allowed cabinet `return_path` for `return: page`.
5. Password, API key and webhook apps use `POST /v1/connectors/{app}/credentials`: the secret is probed (IMAP login, CalDAV `PROPFIND`, Affinity's whoami, a Zapier URL that is HTTPS on Zapier's hook host), stored encrypted, and the same event is written.

### 3.5 Keeping a grant alive

- **Use.** A `ProviderSession` reads the grant's secrets once; if the access token expires within 2 minutes it refreshes first.
- **One refresh at a time.** Transaction A takes a lease: `UPDATE connector_grant_secrets SET refreshing_until = now() + 30 s WHERE grant_id = :id AND refresh_generation = :g AND (refreshing_until IS NULL OR refreshing_until < now())`. The winner calls the token endpoint outside any transaction; transaction B writes the new access token, the new refresh token when one is returned, the expiry and `refresh_generation = g + 1` and clears the lease, conditional on the generation. A loser waits up to 3 s for the generation to move and uses the new token, else answers `ProviderBusy(1 s)`. Transaction B is retried three times inside the lease; a rotated token that still cannot be written is counted (`shosai_connectors_refresh_lost_total`) and the grant becomes `needs_reconnect` at its next use.
- **Outcomes.** `invalid_grant` (or the provider's equivalent) → the grant `needs_reconnect (refresh_refused)`, `connectors.broken` for each of its apps; `invalid_client` → Shosai's own application is misconfigured (an expired Entra secret): an alert, the grants left as they are; 5xx and network failures → transient, the lease released, the failure counted.
- **Calls.** A 401 with a fresh token → one refresh → still 401 → `needs_reconnect (grant_revoked)`. A 403 for a missing scope → that app's connection `needs_reconnect (scope_missing)`. 429 and 503 with `Retry-After` → a cooldown in Valkey for that provider account, or for the whole application when the provider says so, that every process respects, and `ProviderBusy(retry_after)` to the caller.
- **Keep-alive.** For providers whose refresh token dies when unused, `keepalive_at = last refresh + idle expiry − 14 days ± jitter`; the scheduler walks due rows per home (`connectors.keep_alive`, `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`) and refreshes them. This is also how an idle grant's death is noticed (CN-D11). A project in Google's Testing status gives refresh tokens that die seven days after consent whatever is done with them: no keep-alive holds them, so the row says by when to reconnect (`reconnect_by`, step 9) and a refused refresh tells the person once.
- **Disconnect.** Removing an app's connection and writing `connectors.revoked` is one transaction, so every consumer stops at once. If it was the grant's last connection, the grant becomes `revoking` and `connectors.revoke_grant` calls the provider's revocation with the refresh token (Google), the token (Notion, Dropbox, GitLab, Slack, HubSpot, Attio, Yandex with its `device_id`) or the grant (GitHub), then deletes the grant; five attempts with backoff, then it is deleted anyway and the failure counted. Providers without revocation (Microsoft, Atlassian) only forget.
- **Key rotation.** When `FERNET_KEYS_PREVIOUS` is not empty, a weekly job per home re-encrypts secrets under the active key in batches of 500 and reports what it cannot decrypt (`shosai_connectors_undecryptable_secrets{home}`); the runbook removes an old key after a full pass.
- **Atlassian's personal data reporting.** Weekly, connectors reports the Atlassian account ids its grants hold and erases the grants of closed accounts; tasks reports the Jira users it stores (TK-D8).

### 3.6 Provider transport

- One pooled `httpx.AsyncClient` per process for every provider (connect 3 s; read 15 s, 60 s for downloads; redirects not followed; TLS verified); the `Authorization` header and token parameters redacted from every log and span.
- **Limits:** each descriptor names its quotas as GCRA limits (ADR 0011) per application, per provider account or per connection, and concurrency bounds where the provider has them (Outlook: 4 requests in flight per mailbox, a Valkey semaphore); a provider-wide semaphore bounds calls in flight (§12). Both fail open when Valkey is silent — the provider still answers 429.
- **Breaker** per provider in Valkey, counting only 5xx and timeouts across connections; one person's 401, 403 or 429 never opens it.
- **Retries:** reads once with jitter on a network error or 5xx; writes never, unless the provider takes an idempotency key (Leo's write-ahead discipline, Leo §8.4).
- **Telemetry:** span `connectors.provider.call` and `shosai_connectors_provider_requests_total{provider, operation, outcome}` with latency histograms, by operation name — never by URL, id or email.
- **IMAP and SMTP** (step 15): XOAUTH2 or a password, TLS, ≤ 2 sessions per connection per process, closed after 60 s idle, 10 s per command. **CalDAV** (step 16) on the same HTTPX client; an address the person gives is resolved and refused when it points into a private, loopback or link-local range, and the resolved address is pinned for the call (no DNS rebinding).

- A personal connection is listed, used and removed only by its person; a consumer uses it only in that person's scope, or in a workspace while `usable_for` holds (CN-D3).

### 5.2 CASA — what public Gmail waits for

- **Decision (owner, 2026-10-28):** no money for CASA now. Gmail is built and runs for the owner's testers in the "Shosai Mail" project in Google's Testing status; **public Gmail is turned on only when CASA has a budget.** Drive needs no CASA (`drive.file`, CN-D5).
- **Why it is needed for Gmail:** Shosai stores and transmits data of a restricted scope (`gmail.modify`) on its servers, so Google requires a **Cloud Application Security Assessment** by a lab of the App Defense Alliance, renewed **every 12 months** from the lab's letter of assessment, besides Google's own restricted-scope review. Google assigns the level (AL1 or AL2); it can rise with the user base and never falls back.
- **Labs** (ADA's list, 2026-10-28): Bishop Fox, DEKRA, Eydle, Leviathan Security, NCC Group, NetSentries, NowSecure, Prescient Security, TAC Security, ValueMentor.
- **Prices** (no official list; vendors' and third parties' figures, 2024–2026): AL1 (Tier 2) from about **$540 per app** (TAC Security's Basic plan, paid in February 2026, about two working days of lab work; TAC's range $540–1 800 a year), other labs $800–1 500, up to $3 000–6 000 for faster service; AL2 (Tier 3) about **$4 500–8 000** and more. The "$50 000" figure that circulates belongs to old pricing or the most comprehensive tiers.
- **Estimate for Shosai — one project, Gmail only:**
  - **AL1 (the likely level at the start): budget ≈ $1 000–2 000 for the first year** — the lab's fee with a margin for one re-test after fixes — and **≈ $540–1 800 every following year**;
  - **AL2 (if Google assigns it, or later with growth): ≈ $5 000–8 000 a year;**
  - **time: 1–3 weeks of lab work at AL1 (2–4 at AL2) plus fixes — plan 1–2 months — and Google's restricted review of several weeks**, so about **2–3 months from the budget to public Gmail**;
  - engineering work of the review (the scanner's findings, the demo video, the Limited Use statement, the questionnaire) is Shosai's own and needs no budget.

## 6. M1: the draft's grants without reconnecting

1. **The draft's own grants** (`oauth_accounts`: Google and Microsoft calendars, Jira). Each row becomes a grant (provider account, scopes, `provider_meta` with Jira's sites, status: active → `active`; expired or revoked → not carried or `needs_reconnect`) with its ciphertexts **byte for byte** under the same `FERNET_KEY`, and one connection (`google_calendar`, `outlook_calendar`, `jira`) whose **id is the draft's `oauth_accounts.id`**, so the calendar slice's carried references stay valid. The client key is the draft's application for that provider.
2. **Nango's grants** (`connector_connections` and the `nango` schema). At cutover, after the draft stops (M2's freeze) **and Nango is stopped** — it refreshes every connection at least daily and on every credentials read — the import reads `_nango_connections` (AES-256-GCM with `NANGO_ENCRYPTION_KEY`, its IV and tag columns) and `_nango_configs` (each integration's client id) from the database copy, decrypts in memory, and writes Shosai grants under Fernet. Integration keys map to apps: `google-mail` → `gmail` (its `gmail.readonly` and `gmail.send` do not cover `gmail.modify`, so it arrives as `needs_reconnect`, and only a tester can reconnect it while public Gmail is off); `outlook` → `outlook` (mail; its `Calendars.ReadWrite` also serves `outlook_calendar` if the person had no own calendar grant); `google-drive` (`drive.file`) → `google_drive`; `google-drive-kb` (`drive.readonly`) is **not carried** — Shosai's Drive is `drive.file` (CN-D5); its knowledge sources come back when the person picks their files once, and it is not revoked, since it shares the project's grant with the person's calendar; `dropbox`, `dropbox-kb` → `dropbox`; `yandex-disk-kb` → `yandex_disk`; `notion`, `notion-kb` → `notion`; `confluence`, `confluence-kb` → `confluence`; `jira`, `jira-kb` → `jira`; `github`, `github-kb` → `github`; `gitlab`, `gitlab-kb` → `gitlab`; `slack`, `slack-kb` → `slack`; `hubspot` → `hubspot`; the deprecated `google-calendar` is not carried. Each grant keeps its `connection_config` (Atlassian cloud id, Microsoft tenant) in `provider_meta`.
3. **One grant per app and person.** Where the draft has several grants for one app (a Leo and a knowledge integration, or a Nango and an own grant), the import keeps the active one whose scopes cover the app in Shosai, then the newest, and drops the others without revoking (they may share a project with the kept one). Grants of the same Google account and project merge their apps onto one grant.
4. **Scopes.** A grant that does not cover its app's scopes in Shosai is imported as `needs_reconnect (scope_missing)`: the person reconnects that app once (for example a draft Slack grant without the user scopes Shosai needs).
5. **Meeting-push grants** (`meeting_integrations`, CN-D10 d): Notion, HubSpot (with its refresh token), Attio, Affinity (API key) and Zapier (hook URL) become grants and connections; Slack's incoming-webhook URLs are not carried. The push settings (`push_notes`, `push_action_items`, `target`) are not carried: pushing is Leo's proposal with approval (SM-11).
6. **CalDAV** (`calendar_integrations.caldav_*`): a `caldav.yandex.ru` address becomes a `yandex_calendar` connection, any other address a `caldav` connection, the password ciphertext byte for byte; the calendar slice keeps its calendar selection against the connection.
7. **Not carried:** grants of anonymised accounts (DATA_MAPPING D-2), `connector_connections` rows without a Nango connection, Nango's `google-calendar`.
8. **Verification:** every carried ciphertext decrypts inside the runtime (counts per table and client key, no plaintext out of the process); after cutover, one refresh per client key on a non-rotating provider first; the report of grants per app, `needs_reconnect` and dropped rows goes to the owner.
9. **Order at cutover (M2's runbook):** draft read-only and its scheduler stopped → Nango stopped → database copy → import → v2 started. Nothing of the draft may refresh after the copy (Atlassian's rotating chain dies on reuse).

## 8. Risks

- **Public Gmail waits for money, not code** (CN-D5): until CASA has a budget, Gmail reaches at most 100 testers, who reconnect weekly; the estimate is in §5.2.
- **Drive's reach is what the person picks**: a knowledge source does not grow by itself with a folder unless step 12's check shows Google opens a picked folder's files; the cabinet says so where files are chosen.
- **Google's Picker is third-party code in the cabinet**: loaded only when the Picker opens, its origins admitted by the CSP of cabinet pages only (step 12b).
- **Google's sensitive review** sets the date Calendar opens to everyone: it starts with step 9 (free, 3–5 business days).
- **Corporate Outlook** needs each organisation's admin to approve Shosai (Microsoft's default since Nov 2025); publisher verification is the precondition. Personal Microsoft accounts are unaffected.
- **Mail.ru may require Mail Space** for third-party IMAP; if it spreads beyond the experiment, the owner may drop Mail.ru.
- **Marketplace caps**: Slack's history limit without the Marketplace, HubSpot's and Attio's 25 installs without a listing — listings are lead-time work for the owner.
- **Atlassian's 65 000 points an hour** is one pool for all customers of the app (tasks' Jira sweep and Confluence): measured from step 8, alerted at 80%.
- **A rotated refresh token lost** between the provider's answer and the commit breaks one grant: retried inside the lease, counted, told to the person once.
- **Ports before consumers** (CN-D1): a consumer's plan may reshape a port; changes are additive and covered by recorded answers.
- **Provider API changes** (Jira's search answered 410 overnight): recorded answers in CI and the staging check per provider; the broken-connections monitor catches a provider-side change.
- **IMAP from the EU to servers in Russia** adds ≈ 0.3–0.5 s per session; pooled and measured in step 15.
