# memory — slice plan

Prompt: [prompts/S-feature-slice.md](../prompts/S-feature-slice.md), launched through [BACKEND-next-step.md](../prompts/BACKEND-next-step.md). Status: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md), row `memory`. Slice card: [SLICE_MAP.md §4.2.14–18](../SLICE_MAP.md#1418-leo-agent-decisions-memory-proactive-errands). Design, normative: [Leo architecture](../../design/leo/ARCHITECTURE.md) §7 with [ADR 0030](../../adr/0030-leo-memory-v2.md), [ADR 0031](../../adr/0031-leo-context-budget-and-compaction.md), [ADR 0033](../../adr/0033-vectors-pgvector-for-memory-qdrant-for-knowledge-and-meetings.md) and [ADR 0071](../../adr/0071-leo-memory-slice.md). Cabinet features: `memory` (the facts dialog), `settings` (Settings › Memory: the switch and the summary) and `leo` (the candidate's line under an answer), SLICE_MAP §4.3.

`memory` is what Leo knows about the person: their preferences, the facts about them and their work, the people they deal with, their goals and the way they work — each with where it came from, each visible, correctable and forgettable. It learns from what the person says to Leo and from their meetings, in the background; it puts the few facts that matter into a turn within a budget; and it writes the overview the person reads in Settings › Memory.

- **Status:** written on 2026-10-27; **approved by the owner on 2026-10-27 with amendments** ([ADR 0071](../../adr/0071-leo-memory-slice.md)): MM-D2, MM-D3 and MM-D5 as written; MM-D1, MM-D4, MM-D6, MM-D7 and MM-D8 amended by the owner, written into their rows. The documents of §5 were amended with the approval.
- **Dependencies:** identity, F6 and workspaces·core are `done`. `agent` is `in progress` with what memory needs already built: the ports `MemoryRecall` and `ProfileFacts` (declared in step 1, not consulted yet), the context builder (5b), the tool registry (6), the Action Gateway and cards (9a, 9b), `agent.turn.completed` (text and voice turns). `decisions` is `in progress` with its engine shipped (step 3), which its plan names as the point from which memory can start; its step 10 builds `memory.admit` on memory's admission. `notes` is `in progress` with `MeetingReader`, `MeetingFindings` and `notes.meeting.notes_ready` built; its step 24 is closed by this slice. `chats` is `todo`: facts from team chats wait for it (step 12). `studio` is `todo`: memory uses its own prompts and a default model until studio's `PromptResolver` and `ModelRoutes`, as notes does (SM-5).
- **Philosophy check:** the person never has to remind Leo who they are, and never has to tend Leo's memory. Memory works by itself and stays quiet: nothing is announced, nothing interrupts; what Leo is unsure of waits as one line under the answer it came from and in Settings › Memory, until the person looks. Everything Leo uses can be seen with its source, corrected in place or forgotten, and the switch stops it all at once.

### 1.3 Design point

From Leo §2.4 and the target architecture §3 at 10 000 DAU (×10 at 100 000):

- **Recall:** 50 000 turns/day, 2.9/s at peak (29). One query embedding per turn of a person with facts (≈ 100 tokens) and one exact nearest-neighbour read over that person's rows. At ≤ 5 000 facts per person (ADR 0030's limit) a scan of 1 024-dimensional half-precision vectors reads ≈ 10 MB, a few milliseconds; a typical person has hundreds. Budget: the embedding and the read finish within Leo §12's 300 ms of parallel context, or the turn goes on without them.
- **Admission:** one extraction per turn, right after it (≈ 50 000/day; MM-D1's line under the answer needs it within seconds), and one per meeting (19 500/day); ≤ 3 statements per turn, ≤ 5 per meeting; `memory.admit` in shadow on each statement once its gates pass.
- **Facts:** ≈ 5 000 new rows/day after deduplication (assumption: one durable statement per ten turns, two per meeting before candidates are answered) ≈ 1.8 M rows/year; ≈ 2.3 KB per row with a `halfvec(1024)` ≈ 4 GB/year (≈ 40 GB at 100 000 DAU, 80/20 RU/EU), bounded by the purge of ended rows after 180 days.
- **Model cost** (measured in steps 5, 7 and 9; prices of a flash-class model ≈ $0.10/M input, $0.40/M output, embeddings ≈ $0.01/M): turns ≈ 50 000 × (1 300 + 150 output) tokens ≈ $9.5/day; meetings ≈ 19 500 × 1 800 tokens ≈ $4.5/day; summaries ≈ 3 000 × 3 500 tokens ≈ $1.5/day; embeddings on `google/gemini-embedding-2` at $0.20/M — a query per turn of ≈ 100 tokens and the statements — ≈ $1.1/day; `memory.admit` on Jev ≈ 15 000 statements × 1 500 tokens × $0.042/M ≈ $1/day. **≈ $18/day (≈ $540/month), ≈ $5 400/month at 100 000 DAU.**
- **Transactions:** recall adds one read-only transaction per turn on the worker, in parallel with the context's (5 instead of Leo §12's 4); admission ≈ 2 per turn, off the path; upkeep per home once a day.


### 1.4 The embedding model, and knowledge on it

**The owner's choice (MM-D6, 2026-10-27): `google/gemini-embedding-2`** through OpenRouter (Google Vertex endpoints `us` and `global`), $0.20 per million input tokens, 8 192 tokens of context. It returns 3 072 dimensions by default and any of 128–3 072 by Matryoshka truncation, normalizing a truncated vector itself; memory keeps **1 024** (`halfvec(1024)`, ≈ 2 KB per fact). It takes no `task_type`: the task goes into the text — a question as `task: search result | query: {text}`, a stored statement as `title: none | text: {text}`; both prefixes are model-facing text, registered in the English test.

**The measurement** (one run, budget $0.5): done on 2026-10-27 for ≈ $0.10 (≈ $0.02 of embeddings, the rest translation): 80 statements about one person, 40 questions with their relevant statements, 24 pairs of rewordings and 24 pairs of "near misses" — the same subject with another value ("Lives in Moscow" / "Lives in Berlin", "dark mode" / "light mode") — written in English and translated into the other 15 languages by `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash`; 1 024 dimensions asked and returned.

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| recall@12 per language | **0.98–1.00 in all 16** (en, de, fr, es, tr, ar, ja, ko 1.00; ru, it, pt-BR, nl, hi, id, vi, zh-Hant 0.98) |
| recall@12 across languages | question in English over another language's facts 0.96–1.00; question in the language over English facts 1.00 |
| Two different statements of the person | highest similarity **0.867** in any language (p99 ≤ 0.81, median 0.65–0.70) |
| Rewordings of one statement | 0.80–0.99, median 0.955; 98% of them ≥ 0.87 |
| Near misses (same subject, another value) | 0.80–0.98, median 0.92 — **as close as rewordings** |
| Question and its relevant statement | 0.58–0.95, median 0.77; question and an unrelated statement median 0.61, up to 0.79 |

**What follows for the rules** (agent-derived from the numbers, step 1): no cosine threshold separates a rewording from a contradiction — at the draft's 0.92 half of the near misses would have been taken for duplicates and the new value dropped. So similarity says only *the same subject* (**≥ 0.87**, the highest two different statements reach in any language): **only the same words after normalization** (Unicode NFKC, case, punctuation and spacing set aside, in any script) confirm a held fact; a statement on the subject of a current fact becomes a candidate that proposes to replace it, so the person — and, once it acts, `memory.admit` with its neighbours — decides between "the same", "changed" and "both true"; a statement on the subject of a refused fact is not asked again, nor one on the subject of a waiting candidate. Query similarities are compressed too: **the recall floor is 0.55**, which keeps every relevant pair of the set and drops only the clearly unrelated (≈ 3%); ranking and the 12-fact, 600-token budget do the rest. The set and the script become the evaluation command of step 4.

**Knowledge uses the same model by default.** One query embedding then serves Leo's memory recall and knowledge search (Leo §7.2), and a knowledge collection that keeps fewer dimensions — the target architecture's 512 for meeting windows (§3) — takes the first 512 of the same 1 024 and renormalizes, so the query is never embedded twice. **The cost of indexing knowledge on it at the design point** (10 000 DAU; × 10 at 100 000):

| Part | Volume | Tokens | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meeting windows (one per minute, §3) | 0.78 M/day | ≈ 250 per window — a minute of speech, ≈ 150 words at 1.3–1.7 tokens per word across the 16 languages, plus the prefix and the window's heading — ≈ 195 M/day | **≈ $39/day** |
| Documents: changes of connected sources and uploads | assumption: 30% of DAU with sources, ≈ 20 changed documents of ≈ 2 000 tokens a week each | ≈ 17 M/day | ≈ $3.4/day |
| Queries outside Leo's turns (cabinet search) | ≈ 2 per DAU a day | ≈ 0.6 M/day | ≈ $0.1/day |
| **Ongoing** | | ≈ 213 M/day | **≈ $43/day ≈ $1 300/month (≈ $13 000/month at 100 000 DAU)** |
| First index of connected sources, once | assumption: 3 000 people × ≈ 1 000 documents × 2 000 tokens | ≈ 6 B | ≈ $1 200 once per 10 000 DAU, spread over the weeks people connect |

Meeting windows are nine-tenths of it: about 1–2% of the speech-to-text cost at the same point (§3: $2 340–4 680/day). Leo's queries are memory's (§1.3). The knowledge plan confirms these volumes and may choose its own model only with a decision of its own.

**Settled by documents or derived here (agent-derived), no decision needed:**

- The routes are those of Leo §13.2 under `/v1/agent/memory`, extended for the summary, the switch, counts, filters, restore and the imported list (§3.3); `POST /v1/agent/memory` is not built — no screen adds a fact by form; the summary box adds them (MM-D3).
- **Admission runs per turn, right after it**, so a candidate can stand under the answer it came from (MM-D1); the extractor sees the two exchanges before the turn for references, and extracts only from the turn's own message.
- **No model rewrites stored facts in the background.** The draft's nightly LLM consolidation merged and rewrote rows; here supersession happens at admission and by the person, so Leo §7.2's "consolidation" is the daily upkeep: decay, expiry of candidates, purges, departures and the summary of people whose facts changed (§3.6).
- **After auto-save is on, 1 in 20 statements the policy would save is still asked**, and a fact saved automatically and then forgotten or edited by the person within 7 days counts as a disagreement — so the agreement of MM-D1 stays measured and auto-save turns itself off when it drops (§3.4).
- **The profile core is not cached:** it is one statement in the worker's context transaction. Leo §7.1's 10-minute cache would copy a Russian-home person's pinned facts into Valkey in the EU to save a query the transaction makes anyway.
- **Decay is refreshed by the person, not by Leo's use:** accepting, editing, pinning or saying it again. Leo §7.2's "use the model cites" has no signal to read (recalled facts are context, not numbered sources).
- **`no_logs` is meetings' "Don't keep my meetings" (MT-D6):** a meeting not kept never reaches memory; conversation admission depends on the memory switch and cross-border consent only.
- **Leo has no "remember" tool.** "Remember that…" in a conversation is an explicit request admitted seconds later (MM-D1); the profile core tells Leo whether memory is on, so it never promises to remember when it is off. Forgetting is a card (`memory.forget`).
- Memory's model calls are the person's background work (inv-9 cost rows, F6's ceilings), outside their weekly Leo tokens — decisions' DL-D2. The summary box is the person's interactive call, also outside Leo tokens.
- Statements Leo would read as instructions about its behaviour ("always reply in bullet points") are preferences, kept as data under the wrapper, ranked below the policy; anything addressed to Leo to change its rules is not a fact and is skipped (ADR 0030 point 5; with MM-D2 there is no personalization table to route them to).

### 3.3 HTTP interface

Snake_case, ADR 0008 errors, the cabinet's session. No route is gated by plan. A fact from a workspace the person left, a superseded or forgotten fact and another person's fact all answer 404 `memory_fact_not_found`.

| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/agent/memory?status=&kind=&source=&thread_id=&q=&cursor=&limit=` | `{items: MemoryFact[], next_cursor, counts: {active, candidates, imported}}`; candidates first, then active, newest first within each; `q` matches the statement (case-insensitive substring), `kind`, `status` and `source` filter on the server — the dialog's search and filter then cover every page; `thread_id` lists a conversation's pending candidates for the line under its answers; imported candidates are listed only with `source=imported`; `limit` 1–100, default 50 |
| `PATCH /v1/agent/memory/{id}` | `{statement? (1–500), pinned?}` → `MemoryFact`; a new statement supersedes: the answer has a new id and `source.kind: "user_edit"`; editing a candidate accepts it; 422 `memory_fact_invalid` |
| `POST /v1/agent/memory/{id}/accept` | candidate → active (superseding the fact it replaces, when the policy proposed one) → `MemoryFact`; a repeat on the fact it activated answers it unchanged |

| `POST /v1/agent/memory/{id}/restore` | within 24 hours of a forget or of a supersession by the summary box, brings the fact back (closing its successor) → `MemoryFact`; a repeat answers it unchanged; 404 otherwise (MM-D3) |

A `PATCH` with a new statement and `POST …/summary/messages` require an `Idempotency-Key` (core's effect-key ledger, F1 A9): a retry answers the first result instead of failing on a fact its first attempt superseded or applying a message twice.

`MemoryFact` is the cabinet's (`id, kind, statement, status, pinned, source {kind, title}, created_at`) with `source` gaining `thread_id` and `message_id` (a conversation's fact, for the line under its answer) and the kinds `imported` (MM-D8) and `team_chat` (step 12); a meeting's or a chat's title is read live through its owner's reader in the list's transaction, so a renamed meeting shows its new title. The static paths are declared before `/{id}`.

### 3.4 Admission

**Turns.** `agent.turn.completed` queues one task keyed by the run. In the person's scope:

1. **Switches.** Memory off, no cross-border consent (a Russian home), the platform operator or an account in identity's deletion window → nothing is written.
2. **Read** the turn's words through `PersonWords` — the question (≤ 2 000 tokens) and, for references only, the two exchanges before it; a suspicious run is skipped.
3. **Extract** with `MEMORY_MODEL`, structured output, English prompt, the statements in the person's language: at most 3 `{statement (≤ 300 characters, third person), kind, explicit, sensitive, about_person}`. Questions, requests, tasks, plans with a date, opinions about things and anything addressed to Leo to change its rules are not statements.
4. **Neighbours.** Embed the statements (one call); the 5 nearest of the person's facts — current, candidates, and those forgotten or turned down in the last 180 days.
5. **Rules before any judge** (§1.4): on the subject of a fact means similarity ≥ 0.87. A current fact on the subject with the same words → confirmed (`confirmations + 1`, `last_confirmed_at`); a pending candidate on the subject → skipped; a forgotten or turned-down fact on the subject and not explicit → skipped; the nearest current fact on the subject is what a candidate proposes to replace (`replaces`).
6. **Judge.** Once decisions' step 10 ships `memory.admit`, every statement is decided by it (in shadow, recorded); the outcome memory acts on is:
   - **auto-save closed** (the gate below not met, or the policy not active): the rule — explicit → active, confirmed by the person; otherwise → candidate within the daily cap;
   - **auto-save open:** `store` of a statement that is not sensitive → active, not confirmed (taint per MM-D7), except 1 in 20 asked as a candidate; `supersede` of the same → active, closing the neighbour; `store` or `supersede` of a sensitive one, and `ask` → candidate (carrying `replaces` for a supersession); `skip` → nothing; explicit requests stay active and confirmed whatever the outcome.
7. **Write** in one transaction under the person's advisory lock: the facts with their space, source (thread and message, or chat and message), confidence, sensitivity, `explicit`, `confirmed`, `source_taint` (the run's final taint); the daily cap counted inside the lock; the summary marked stale; the "has memories" bit set. A redelivered task writes nothing twice (the run's effect key).

**The person's answer as the outcome** (MM-D1). Remember, Don't remember, an edit, and — for a fact saved automatically — a forget or an edit within 7 days are reported through `observe(MEMORY_ADMIT, home, statement, answer)`. Decisions' daily reliability pass counts them per band and language.

**The auto-save gate** (MM-D1, numbers decided by the owner; checked by decisions' step 10 on promotion and daily):

- the policy passed its synthetic gates in all 16 languages and its reference set (Leo §6.4, ADR 0069) and its two weeks of shadow;
- **agreement ≥ 95%** — of the statements the policy would have saved automatically (`store`, not sensitive) and the person answered, the share the person kept (Remember, or an edit kept);
- **in every language with at least 200 such answered statements** in the last 90 days (decision records live 90 days), and at least one language qualifies; at 200 an observed 95% has a 95% interval of about ±3 points. A language below 200 does not block, and is covered by the synthetic gate;
- **re-checked daily:** any qualifying language below 95% over the last 28 days (with at least 100 answered) closes auto-save — statements become candidates again — and opens a ticket.

**Meetings** (MM-D5). `notes.meeting.notes_ready` with revision 1 → a task keyed by the meeting: the same switches for the meeting's owner; the meeting read through `MeetingReader.facts` and `MeetingFindings.of` as the owner; at most 5 statements from the recollections prompt; neighbours and rules as above; every statement a candidate with the label `meeting:speech`, its source `{meeting, finding?}` and the meeting's space; no line under a Leo answer (it has none); the policy's shadow records them too.

**Team chats** (MM-D4, step 12). A Leo turn from a team chat ("@Leo …", `ChatTurns`) is admitted as a turn, its source the chat and the message, its space the chat's workspace.

### 3.5 Recall, the profile core and the tools

- **Profile core** (segment 4): read in the worker's context transaction — the switch, then pinned facts, then current `preference` facts by retention, in a fixed order (by id) while ≤ 400 of segment 4's 600 tokens hold; wrapped as one block with the HMAC boundary (MM-D7); one line says whether memory is on. The block's bytes stay the same until a pinned fact or a preference changes. Facts from a space the person left are absent. A pinned fact the person never confirmed raises the run's taint like any other (pinning is a confirmation, so this happens only for imported ones pinned by migration — MM-D2's are the person's own settings and count as confirmed).
- **Recall** (segment 7): started by the worker in parallel with the context's read; skipped when the switch is off, consent is missing or the "has memories" bit is clear. The query — the question and the first 300 characters of the previous answer — is embedded once (deadline 250 ms); one read-only transaction reads the person's current facts of the current embedding model in their active spaces, exact nearest 40 by cosine, keeps those above the similarity floor (set in step 4), ranks by similarity × retention × confidence, packs ≤ 12 facts and ≤ 600 tokens, skipping a fact that does not fit rather than stopping. The whole recall has a 350 ms deadline; late, the turn goes on without it (counted). Facts are wrapped per run with their label; confirmed facts leave the taint as it is, others raise it to at least `external` (MM-D7).
- **The agent's side** (built in step 4, additive): `PROFILE_FACTS` in the context transaction, `MEMORY_RECALL` as a parallel task, the HMAC boundary for segment 4, `taint_after` reading each fact's `raises_taint`, the `memory` toolset offered by the profile's switch.
- **Tools.** `memory.recall` reads like recall but with the model's query and up to 20 facts, each with its source and date, for "what do you know about…" and "why do you think…". `memory.forget` plans the exact fact ids (current, the person's, in their spaces), previews each statement read by the server in the person's 16 languages (`PreviewTexts`), and on approval forgets them in memory's transaction with the item key; a restore within 24 hours is its compensation.

### 3.6 Data (each home)

All tables isolate rows by `user_id` (RLS's personal policy, ADR 0053); none is shareable.

- **`memory_facts`:** `id` UUIDv7, `user_id`, `org_id` (the source's space, MM-D4), `kind` (`preference`, `fact`, `relationship`, `goal`, `procedure`), `statement` (≤ 500), `subject_refs` (uuid[], empty until `commitments`), `source_kind` (`user_statement`, `meeting`, `team_chat`, `settings`, `user_edit`, `imported`), `source_ref` (jsonb of ids: thread and message, meeting and finding, or chat and message), `thread_id` (a conversation's, for the line under its answer), `confidence`, `sensitivity` (`normal`, `sensitive`), `explicit`, `confirmed` (MM-D7), `source_taint` (`none`, `external`), `status` (`candidate`, `active`, `forgotten`), `ended_reason` (`forgot`, `turned_down`, `expired`, `forgot_all`), `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `superseded_by`, `replaces` (a candidate's proposed predecessor), `pinned`, `confirmations`, `last_confirmed_at`, `embedding halfvec(1024)` (null until embedded), `embedding_model`, `erase_after`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. Checks in PostgreSQL: the closed sets, statement length, a candidate never pinned, `superseded_by` only with `valid_to`, a confirmed fact never `external` by itself. Indexes: `(user_id) WHERE status = 'active' AND valid_to IS NULL` (recall and profile — MM-D6), `(user_id, status, created_at DESC, id)` (lists, the daily cap), `(thread_id) WHERE status = 'candidate'` (the line under an answer), partial on `erase_after`, partial on `(status, updated_at)` for the purge.
- **`memory_settings`:** `user_id` PK, `enabled` (default true), `changed_at` — the monitor reads facts written after a switch-off.
- **`memory_summaries`:** `user_id` PK, `sections` (jsonb), `suggestions` (jsonb), `language`, `facts_digest` (the current facts it was written from), `written_at`, `stale`.
- **pgvector:** the `vector` extension created in both homes by the migration role; the `pgvector` Python package (latest stable) for the SQLAlchemy type. The local and test servers' image (`pgvector/pgvector:0.8.6-pg18`) has it; the managed homes' support is checked with F4's resources before staging.
- **Valkey:** `memory:has:{user}` — the "has memories" bit (Leo §10), set on a current fact's write, cleared when none is left, 24 h TTL; a missing bit is read as "yes".
- **Upkeep**, daily per home, each part keyed by its day: facts whose retention falls below 0.15 expire (pinned never; half-life fact 180 d, relationship 180 d, goal 30 d, preference 365 d, procedure 365 d, stretched by confirmations and doubled when explicit); candidates older than 30 days leave (`expired`), imported ones excepted (MM-D8); rows ended or superseded more than 180 days ago are purged; rows past `erase_after` are erased; team-chat facts past their chat's retention are erased (step 12); stale summaries of people with memory on are rewritten on a cursor; facts of an older embedding model are re-embedded on a cursor.

### 3.7 Flows

1. **A turn.** The worker reads the profile core with the context and starts recall beside it; the model sees pinned facts and preferences before the cache breakpoint and the recalled facts with the turn. At the turn's end, `agent.turn.completed`.
2. **Admission** seconds after the turn (§3.4). A new candidate appears quietly as one line under that answer — "Leo may remember: «…» · Remember · Don't remember" — which the panel reads when it opens the thread and once a few seconds after each completed turn (the admission's time, measured in step 5; proactive's `/v1/ws/user` may replace that read with a push); and in the list in Settings › Memory. Nothing else announces it.
3. **The person's controls.** Edit supersedes; accept activates (superseding what it replaces); forget and turn down end a fact; forget all ends every one and empties the summary; the switch stops reading and writing at once and keeps the data. Every answer to a statement the policy judged is reported to it.
4. **The summary.** Rewritten from the current facts (≤ 200, pinned first, by retention; unconfirmed ones wrapped) in the person's interface language: 2–5 titled sections without markup and ≤ 3 suggested questions, validated before it is kept; stale after any change, rewritten by the daily upkeep or when the person opens it stale and the last write is over an hour old (queued; the open never waits), or at once by `refresh`.
5. **Departures and deletions** (MM-D4): `workspaces.member.removed` sets `erase_after` (7 days) on the person's facts of that space, a restored membership clears it, `workspaces.workspace.deleted` erases at once; `notes.meeting.deleted` hides the meeting's facts with the meeting's purge date, `notes.meeting.restored` clears it; a chat's disconnection erases its facts, its retention erases them by date (step 12).
6. **Account deletion and transfer** through the account data handler; export lists facts with their kind, status, source and dates, and the summary.

## 6. Risks

- **Extraction quality differs by language.** The 16-language suite gates step 5; a language below the others is visible there before any person sees it; the agreement per language is the auto-save gate.
- **Candidates become a chore.** The cap of 3 a day, the 30-day expiry and "never ask again" bound them; auto-save takes the confident ones once the gate opens; if acceptance stays low (ADR 0030's 70%), the extractor or the rule changes before the cap rises.
- **The gate never opens.** Few people answer candidates, so no language reaches 200 answers in 90 days; memory then stays on candidates — the safe side — and the minimum is revisited with the numbers.
- **Per-turn extraction costs most of the budget.** Measured per turn on the pilot (step 5); F6's ceilings bound it; if it is too dear, a cheaper extractor is evaluated through the same suite.
- **A provider changes the embedding model behind its name.** The model is pinned by its exact id; a change is a re-embedding pass, never a silent mix (the draft's lesson).
- **Forgotten text is kept 180 days** for "never ask again" and history (Leo §7.2); account deletion erases it at once.
- **The prefix cache.** Segment 4's bytes are tested for stability (step 4); a preference admitted mid-thread changes it once.
