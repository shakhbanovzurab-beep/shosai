# decisions — slice plan

Prompt: [prompts/S-feature-slice.md](../prompts/S-feature-slice.md), launched through [BACKEND-next-step.md](../prompts/BACKEND-next-step.md). Status: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md), row `decisions`. Slice card: [SLICE_MAP.md §4.2.14–18](../SLICE_MAP.md#1418-leo-agent-decisions-memory-proactive-errands). Design, normative: [Leo architecture](../../design/leo/ARCHITECTURE.md) §3.2, §6, §7.4, §10, §11 with [ADR 0026](../../adr/0026-leo-decision-layer-on-jev.md) and [ADR 0068](../../adr/0068-leo-agent-slice.md) AG-D7. Cabinet feature: none of its own; the action card of `leo` shows its result.

`decisions` is Leo's Decision Layer. It answers the judgement calls the rest of Leo must not make by hand — which tools a turn needs, how carefully a card must ask, whether foreign text tries to instruct Leo, and later what to remember, whether and when to speak first, and which mail needs the person — as typed outcomes from versioned policies over Jev, a calibrated decision model, behind deterministic rules that only ever make an outcome more careful. Every decision has a fallback and a record; no decision grants a right, removes a confirmation or writes text.

- **Status:** written on 2026-10-26; **approved by the owner on 2026-10-26 with amendments** ([ADR 0069](../../adr/0069-decision-layer-slice.md)): DL-D2, DL-D3, DL-D4, DL-D6 and DL-D7 as written; DL-D1 decided by the owner's rule once Jev's published terms were compared with Leo's models (§1.4: not worse, so Jev runs under Leo's cross-border consent); DL-D5 and DL-D8 amended by the owner. The documents of §5 were amended with the approval.
- **Dependencies:** identity is `done` (the cross-border consent reader); F6 is `done` — the provider port's `decide` through OpenRouter's Decisions API with the pinned `typesafe/jev-1.13`, the contract test on a recorded answer, a deadline per call, one cost row per effect key, breakers and provider slots. The agent's ports it binds (`TurnRouter`, `FrictionJudge`) exist since agent step 1 and are not consulted yet. Not needed to start: `memory`, `proactive`, `mail`, `hubs`, `studio` — their points come with them (steps 10–14). **Memory, proactive and mail depend on this slice:** memory can start once step 3 has shipped the engine.
- **Philosophy check:** the person never sees the Decision Layer and never tends it — no screen, no setting. It works by keeping Leo quieter and more precise: the tools a question needs, a warning only where a write is risky (a warning on every card would teach people to ignore warnings), a plain note when a page or a meeting tried to instruct Leo. When it is unsure, slow, or not allowed to read, Leo behaves exactly as without it.

### 1.2 The draft, for lessons

The draft never had a Decision Layer or Jev; its judgements were scattered, unmeasured and, in places, language-dependent ([AUDIT.md](../../design/leo/AUDIT.md)). The points that replace them come with their consumers; their fallbacks are the draft's rules where the draft had any.

| Draft fact (audit) | Rule in v2 |
|---|---|
| "Speak now?" was a static urgency per kind; the stored confidence was ignored; no notion of attention (P1, `restraint.py`) | `proactive.value` and `proactive.moment` (step 11); the draft's `restraint.decide` is their fallback |
| The feedback loop was dead: no rating UI, dismissal not recorded, a value rate that could not be computed (P2) | Every consulted decision is recorded and its consumer reports the outcome (DL-D7); reliability per band is computed from ids and numbers |
| A 16-language "remember" regex and word lists decided memory; CJK penalised by length (M5) | No word list, no branch by language anywhere; a policy runs everywhere or nowhere (the 16-language gate) |
| 130 tool schemas every round, selected by tariff only (T1) | `turn.route` ranks toolsets by need once the catalog outgrows segment 2 (DL-D3); `load_toolset` stays the recovery |
| No judgement of a write's risk beyond a destructive flag; mail summaries made before the gates that rejected them (A-series, P7) | `action.friction` may only add a warning (DL-D4); proactive prepares content only for kept candidates (its slice) |
| Mail importance by threshold rules (`importance.py`) | `mail.triage` (step 12) with `importance.decide` as its fallback, two independent signals kept |

### 1.3 Design point

At 10 000 DAU (100 000 in brackets), from Leo §2.4 and the agent's measurements:

| Point | When it is asked | Requests/day | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `turn.route` | only when the rule cannot fit every allowed toolset in segment 2 — not today (22 tools fit); about every turn once wave 2–3's toolsets land | 0 now; ≈ 50 000 later (500 000) | 350 ms, parallel with the context |
| `action.friction` | per proposal the rule left standard (an untainted run, a reversible write) | ≈ 1 500 (15 000) | 400 ms, before the card |
| `content.injection` | per piece of labelled text entering a run's context (a result digest or a `read_result` page, ≤ 1 500 tokens each), the person's uploads and attachments included (DL-D5) | ≈ 85 000 (850 000) | 300 ms, off the round's path |

- **Jev:** ≈ 140 000 requests/day at ≈ 1 700 input tokens ≈ **$10/day** (≈ $100 at 100 000 DAU) before memory, proactive and mail add theirs (Leo §6.5's $19/day in all). Peak ≈ 8 requests/s (80), ≈ 3 in flight (≈ 25) of OpenRouter's 292 slots. Shadow versions with their own questions add their requests while they run.
- **Records:** ≈ 140 000 rows/day × ≈ 300 B with indexes ≈ 42 MB/day ≈ 3.8 GB over 90 days (≈ 38 GB at 100 000 DAU, 80/20 RU/EU); written in batches of one statement per second per process and home (DL-D7).
- **Latency:** the engine's own work (version from a 60 s cache, pre-rules, a state of ≤ 2 K tokens, bands) is under 2 ms; the rest is Jev and the port's Valkey round trips (reserve, slot, settle ≈ 3–5 ms). The cost row's transaction leaves the path (DL-D2).

### 1.4 Jev's terms against Leo's models (DL-D1)

The owner's rule (2026-10-26): if Jev's terms on training, retention and region are not worse than those of Leo's main models through OpenRouter, Jev works under the same cross-border consent as Leo (nfr-14). Leo's models are those staging names (`deploy/staging/render-env.py`): `google/gemini-3.8-flash` for answers, notes and utility work, `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` as the fallback. Shosai's calls set no provider preference and no `provider.zdr`, so OpenRouter may send them to any endpoint of the model. Read on 2026-10-26:

| | Jev (`typesafe/jev-1.13`) | Gemini 3.8 Flash | Claude Haiku 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endpoints on OpenRouter | one: TypeSafe (`typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`) | Google Vertex (global, us; flex, priority) and Google AI Studio (standard, flex, priority) | Anthropic, Google Vertex (global, us, europe), Amazon Bedrock (global, us-east-1, eu-west-1), Azure (global, us), Claude Platform on AWS |
| On OpenRouter's zero-data-retention list (`/api/v1/endpoints/zdr`) | **its only endpoint is** | only the Vertex endpoints; **AI Studio's are not** | Vertex and Bedrock; **Anthropic's own, Azure's and Claude Platform on AWS's are not** |
| Training | TypeSafe: "We don't use your inputs or outputs to train our models"; "Jev is not trained on customer requests or responses". A ZDR endpoint "will not store your data for any period of time" and so cannot train on it (OpenRouter) | per endpoint: none on the ZDR ones; the others by their provider's terms | as Gemini |
| Retention | none on OpenRouter's endpoint (ZDR). TypeSafe's own help centre leaves retention to "applicable agreements" and offers ZDR to enterprise customers; through OpenRouter the endpoint is listed as ZDR | none on Vertex; AI Studio may keep prompts by its terms | none on Vertex and Bedrock; the others may keep prompts by their terms |
| Region | TypeSafe publishes no processing region (its FAQ leaves the question unanswered); third-party summaries say the United States | Vertex `global` and `us`, AI Studio: outside Russia, not pinned to the EU | `global`, `us` and two EU regions, chosen by OpenRouter: not pinned |
| OpenRouter itself | prompts and completions are not logged unless input and output logging is turned on (it is opt-in); in-region EU routing only on its Business and Enterprise plans, not used | same | same |

**Verdict: not worse.** On training Jev is equal to the best of the main models; on retention it is stricter, since every Jev call lands on a ZDR endpoint while Leo's answers may land on AI Studio or Anthropic's own endpoint; on region it is the same kind of transfer — out of Russia, to endpoints Shosai does not pin to the EU — which the cross-border consent already covers for Leo. **So Jev reads personal data under Leo's consent, with no separate legal gate,** once a policy has passed its synthetic and reference gates and two weeks of shadow (DL-D1, DL-D8). The general legal review of AI providers as subprocessors (target §22) covers Jev as it covers the others.

**Keeping it true** (agent-derived): the comparison rests on the endpoint staying on the ZDR list, which OpenRouter "automatically updates when there are changes to a provider's data policy". A daily task of this slice (`decisions.check_zero_retention`, EU home) reads that list and the pinned model's endpoints over core's OpenRouter wire and keeps the answer in Valkey for three days; when an endpoint is off the list, or no read succeeded for three days, every point runs its rule with the reason `not_zdr` (step 3), shown by `shosai_decision_zero_retention`; its alert comes with the other tickets (step 8).

Sources: [OpenRouter, Zero Data Retention](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr); the public lists `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr` and `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/<model>/endpoints`; [OpenRouter, input and output logging](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/input-output-logging); [OpenRouter, logging and in-region routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/logging); [TypeSafe, enterprise](https://typesafe.ai/enterprise); [TypeSafe help, "How is submitted data used or retained?"](https://help.typesafe.ai/articles/5974925716-how-is-submitted-data-used-or-retained). TypeSafe's DPA and subprocessor list were not published on the pages read.

### 3.3 The engine

1. **Version in force.** The active and shadow versions of the point and their threshold revisions, from the EU home's `decision_policies` and `decision_thresholds`, cached 60 s per process; on a failed refresh the last good copy is kept; with none ever loaded the point runs its rule. A version is active only once promoted with a passing report (§3.5).
2. **Pre-rules,** deterministic, in this order: the rule's own outcome (the consumer passes it) and the policy's short-circuit (it cannot change the outcome: nothing to route, already a warning, already suspicious) → counted, not recorded; no active or shadow version → `policy_off`; the pinned Jev endpoint not on OpenRouter's ZDR list at the last check, or no check for three days → `not_zdr` (DL-D1); a Russian-home person without cross-border consent → `no_consent`; the process's in-flight cap reached → `busy`.
3. **State.** JSON only: typed features (numbers computed in code — counts, sizes, levels — and closed enums) and text in string fields named for what they hold (`request`, `action`, `text`), each cut to its share of ≈ 2 K tokens. JSON's escaping keeps text inside its field, so no wrapper marker is needed; the field names are the provenance. The state's keyed hash (HMAC-SHA-256 with the home's key, 16 bytes) goes to the record, never the state.
4. **Jev,** through `CoreServices.ai.decide` with the effect key `decision:<decision_id>`, the payer of DL-D2 and a port deadline of three times the policy's (≤ 2 s). The engine waits only the policy's deadline; the call itself is shielded, so a late answer still lands — its cost row is written and it is recorded as `late` for calibration, while the caller already has the fallback. A shadow version whose questions are the active one's re-bands the same answer at no cost; one with other questions sends its own request alongside.
5. **Bands.** Each answer maps to an outcome through the revision's thresholds with a margin; an answer inside a margin takes the more careful side. A `choice` or `score` answer uses its probabilities, never only its top value.
6. **Post-rules** may only make the outcome more careful; the final outcome is `max(rule, judged)` in the point's order, a property tested with Hypothesis over every point.
7. **Record** (DL-D7), metrics and the span `decision.<point>` (policy, version, source, outcome, latency; no state). A call that runs past its deadline is recorded once it ends, its late probabilities beside the rule's outcome it returned (`late`). The outcome returns with its record's id, for logs and traces; the consumer reports what followed by the subject (`observe`).

Failures of the provider — timeout, refusal, a broken contract, a breaker open, no slot, a spend ceiling — end in the fallback with their reason (`deadline`, `provider_error`, `contract`, `budget`); none reaches the caller as an error.

### 3.5 Calibration and promotion

- **Datasets** (DL-D8): `infrastructure/datasets/<point>/<language>.jsonl` — facts, text fields and the label; **the reference set** `infrastructure/datasets/<point>/reference/{ru,en}.jsonl` — ≥ 50 cases per language with the human label and the generator's label kept apart (the person labels without seeing it); a recorded run of Jev over both (`probabilities.jsonl`, the snapshot named) for CI.
- **Gates** per version and revision. *Synthetic:* ≥ 50 examples per band and language; expected calibration error ≤ 0.05; the per-language accuracy floor stated in the policy (no language below it); for `turn.route`, the share of turns whose called toolsets were all offered ≥ 95% on the set (the 5% `load_toolset` alert of Leo §8.2). *Reference:* the generator's labels agree with the human ones on ≥ 90% of the cases; the policy's accuracy on each reference language is within 10 points of its synthetic accuracy in that language and at least its floor; its careful outcome is found on at least the floor's share of the cases that need it.
- **`calibrate`** runs Jev over both sets (live; neither holds a person's data), fits the thresholds on the synthetic set for the policy's target precision per band, checks them on the reference, writes a `draft` revision with its report (numbers only: counts, ECE, accuracy per language, reference agreement, the datasets' digests). **`promote`** moves a version and revision to `shadow` or `active` only with a report that passes every gate and, for `active`, after its shadow window (two weeks or 5 000 decisions, whichever is later, Leo §6.4); one active and one shadow per point at most, enforced by the database. **`retire`** returns the point to its rule at once.
- **Online** (step 8): a daily pass per home joins yesterday's records with their outcomes into reliability counts per point, version, band and language, kept in the EU home (`decision_reliability`, counts only); `report` shows them beside the fallback's precision, which is the baseline ADR 0026's "revisit" rule compares against.

The names follow the target architecture (`agent_decisions`) although the owner is `decisions`; the account data spec covers both tables as `expires`.

### 3.7 Flows

1. **A turn whose catalog overflows** (step 6). The run starts `turn.route` with the rule's `Route` and the question, in parallel with the context's reads; the first model request waits for it at most until its deadline. The run's row keeps the routed set as today; at the run's end the agent reports the toolsets called.
2. **A proposal** (step 5). The gateway plans and previews the write; the rule gives the friction; if standard and Jev is cleared, `action.friction` judges; `combine` keeps the stronger; the card shows it. When the person approves or rejects, or the card expires, the agent reports it.
3. **Foreign text** (step 7). When a result or a page is wrapped with an untrusted label, the agent starts `content.injection` for that piece and goes on with the round. Before a write's card and at the run's end it awaits the pending judgements (each within its own deadline); a positive one raises the run's taint to suspicious, which the card states.

### 3.8 Monitors and telemetry

Per [docs/observability.md](../../observability.md): `decision_requests_total{point,source,outcome}`, `decision_latency_seconds{point,source}`, `decision_fallback_total{point,reason}`, `decision_late_total{point}`, `decision_records_lost_total`; the span `decision.<point>`; logs in Russian with English keys, no text. **Invariant monitor** (§16.4, pages on any): `decision_unsafe_outcome_total` — an outcome less careful than its rule — must stay 0. **Tickets:** the pinned Jev endpoint off OpenRouter's ZDR list, or the list unread for three days (DL-D1); from the first active policy, Jev answers within the deadline for ≥ 95% of a point's asked decisions over a day, and a point's fallback share above 20% for a day while active. Dashboard panels: Jev share, fallbacks by reason, latency, cost per day, reliability per band.

### 3.9 Not in decisions

Confirmation (the effect class, inv-12), access (each slice's policy, inv-14), the next tool inside a round (the model), any text; the skill body scan (with studio's composition of a prompt, agent step 18); points whose consumer is not built (steps 10–14).
