# notes — slice plan

Prompt: [prompts/S-feature-slice.md](../prompts/S-feature-slice.md), launched through [BACKEND-next-step.md](../prompts/BACKEND-next-step.md). Status: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md). Slice card: [SLICE_MAP.md §4.2.5](../SLICE_MAP.md#5-notes). Named `meetings` until [ADR 0057](../../adr/0057-notes-and-meetings-slice-names.md) (2026-10-20): the cabinet keeps Notes and Meetings, and the backend slices are named after them; the decisions keep their `MT-D*` ids.

`notes` turns every recorded meeting into something the person never has to hold in their head: the transcript with speakers, Leo's write-up by a template, the findings with their evidence (key points, decisions, action items, promises, risks, questions), the mind map, the person's own notes synced with the desktop, folders and a share link. It owns the meeting's lifecycle from start to processing — also when the client never says the meeting ended (inv-7) — and the transcript store the live capture writes through. It comes in wave 1 right after files, because capture writes into it, Leo's agent reads it, and workspaces, commitments, hubs, tasks and knowledge build on its events and read contracts.

- **Status:** written on 2026-10-19 and approved by the owner the same day: MT-D6, MT-D7, MT-D9, MT-D11 and the transcript's partition key as proposed, MT-D1, MT-D2, MT-D3, MT-D4, MT-D5, MT-D8, MT-D10 with the owner's amendments (§2); ARCHITECTURE §7, §8.1 and §10, SLICE_MAP §4.2.5, DATA_MAPPING §5.4 and the D1 README amended with the approval (§5); ADR 0056 is written in step 1.
- **Dependencies:** identity, workspaces·core, billing·entitlements and F6 are `done`. **files is `in progress`**: its last open item is a staging observation (the abandoned uploads started on 2026-10-18 gone within a day, files step 11); every files contract notes uses (`Uploads.claim`, purpose `recording`, ADR 0055's keys and bucket rules) shipped in files' steps 1–3. Implementation starts when files is `done`, or earlier if the owner allows.
- **Philosophy check:** the person records or uploads and does nothing else. The meeting ends itself when the recording stops, the notes appear within minutes with every claim linked to the moment it was said, action items go to the one task list without retyping, and a colleague or a client gets one link. Nothing asks for attention: processing is shown as a quiet status on the card, a failure says plainly why and what to do, and the only setting is whether meetings are kept at all.

### 3.3 Data (each home)

- `sessions` (meetings): `id`, `user_id`, `org_id`, `access` (shareable), `title` (≤ 256), `status` (`active`, `completed`, `failed`), `source` (`live`, `import`; `room` with the `meetings` slice's video calls), `started_at` (immutable), `ended_at`, `seconds` (set at the end, at most 6 h; a live meeting counts `now − started_at`; a desktop recording raises it to the recording's length when longer, MT-D3), `client_device_id`, `kept` (false for no-logs, MT-D6), `folder_id`, `template_id`, `line_count`, `indexed_upto_seq`, `notes_revision`, `latest_run_id`, `deleted_at` (MT-D8), `transcript_archive_key` and `transcript_archive_lines` (both or neither, step 20), timestamps. Indexes: owner and start, partial on active, partial on hidden for the purge, partial on unarchived by start for the archive.
- `transcript_segments`: `(session_id, seq)` unique, **partitioned by month of the meeting's start** (`meeting_started_at`, a copy of the immutable `sessions.started_at`): a partitioned table's unique key must hold its partition key, and this one is a function of `session_id`, so the key is truly `(session_id, seq)` and every meeting lives in one partition (reads, deletion and the archive touch one); `org_id`, `start_ms` (offset from the start), `duration_ms`, `speaker_idx`, `text` (≤ 2 000), `origin` (`live`, `recording`), `run_id`. Index `(session_id, start_ms, seq)`. Partitions kept until the archive step moves them (§8.1): a year after its UTC month ends, each meeting's lines go to the home's bucket and the partition is dropped (step 20).
- `meeting_speakers`: `(session_id, idx)`, `role` (`owner`, `other`, `numbered`), talk time, `person_id` (commitments, later).
- `meeting_participants`: `id`, `session_id`, `org_id`, `email` (normalised), `name`, `source` (`added`, `calendar`, `speaker`); unique `(session_id, email)`.
- `meeting_notes`: `session_id` PK, `org_id`; the write-up (`write_up` JSONB of sections `{kind, heading_code | title, text | finding ids}`, `write_up_template`, `write_up_run_id`, `findings_run_id` — the run whose findings it lists, kept by a rewrite (step 6) —, `write_up_automatic`; a migrated note is one Markdown section, and the native note route renders the write-up to Markdown as `enhancedMarkdown`); the map (`map` JSONB tree).
- `meeting_own_notes` (MT-D12): `id`, `session_id`, `user_id` — the writer, not necessarily the meeting's owner — `markdown` ≤ 256 KiB, `version`, `client`, `updated_at`; unique `(session_id, user_id)`; personal by nature (`user_isolation`): a row is its writer's alone; it goes with its meeting.
- `meeting_findings`: `id`, `session_id`, `org_id`, `run_id`, `revision`, `kind` (`key_point`, `decision`, `action_item`, `promise`, `risk`, `question`), `text`, `topic`, `owner_speaker_idx`, `owner_name`, `due_on`, `priority` (action items only, MT-D13, step 26), `start_ms`, `end_ms`, `position`; a stage writes its run's set in one transaction, once (step 6: a run's findings are its natural key, the revision orders them).
- `meeting_folders` (`id`, `user_id`, `name`, `color`), `meeting_templates` (`id`, `user_id`, `name`, `description`, `icon`, `sections` JSONB; ≤ 50), `meeting_settings` (`user_id` PK, `keep_meetings`): personal by nature (`user_isolation`).
- `meeting_processing_runs`: `id`, `session_id`, `user_id`, `org_id`, `source` (`live`, `import`, `recording`, `regenerate`), `idempotency_key` unique per meeting (`live` for end and sweep alike, `import:{upload}`, `recording`, `regenerate:{key}`), `state`, `stage_state` JSONB `{stage: {status, attempt, error_code, finished_at, data}}`, the audio pointer (`audio_key`, `multipart_id`, `content_type`, `size_bytes`, `duration_ms`, `lead_ms`, `expires_at`), the raw transcript and turns pointers (O4, O5), `objects_pending` (pointers whose deletion failed), `error_code`, `layout` (a rewrite's template and its sections as they were at the request, step 6), timestamps. Partial indexes on unfinished runs and pending objects.
- `meeting_result_shares`: `session_id` PK (one link), `token` unique (43-character base64url, migrated tokens verbatim), `org_id`, `show_transcript`, `show_names`, `allow_comments`, `expires_at`, timestamps. `meeting_share_comments`: UUIDv7, `session_id`, `author` (≤ 60), `text` (≤ 2 000), `created_at`; no IP or id stored.
- A public read has no person, and a shareable table's personal rows are guarded by the access policy in the query, not by row-level security (WS-D2), so a policy keyed on the token would narrow nothing. Instead `meetings_shared_meeting(token)`, a function of the tables' owner, answers past row-level security only the meeting and the company of the link with that token; the page is then read in that company's scope with no person, the token and the link's state checked in the same query, and comments are inserted the same way. The token is looked up in both homes in parallel (reads only; a migrated or transferred link answers from its home). *Changed in step 2 from an `app.share_token` policy, for the reason above.*

### 3.4 Flows

1. **Start** (`POST /v1/sessions`, capture's `bind`). In the person's transaction: `MinutesAllowance.check(device)` (402, 403), the person's other active meetings on the same device closed, the meeting created with `kept` from the setting. The socket binds with `LiveMeetings.bind`.
2. **Live transcript.** Capture assigns `seq` and the phrase start, queues the line in Valkey, shows it, and its drain calls `TranscriptStore.append` in batches; replays conflict and are ignored; a meeting not kept stores nothing.
3. **End.** `PATCH /v1/sessions/{id}` with `completed` or `failed`, `POST /v1/me/sessions/{id}/end`, the silence sweep or the 6-hour ceiling (MT-D2): one conditional update `active → completed` with the server's `ended_at` and `seconds`; in the same transaction the run `live` is created (an existing one is answered) when the meeting is kept and has speech, `notes.meeting.ended` is written, and the inv-6 check compares the stored `seq`s with the highest shown (`shosai_notes_transcript_lines_missing_total`, `StaysAtMost(0)`). A meeting not kept is purged at once, its minutes kept as a tombstone. A reconciliation sweep picks up an ended, kept meeting with speech and no run (inv-7).
4. **Run.** A run is a sequence of stages on the `meeting` queue, one task per stage, under the task's lease: mark the stage running and commit, call the provider through the port with the effect key `meetings:{run}:{stage}:{part}` (`ESSENTIAL` for transcription and diarization, `STANDARD` for the write-up; origin `BACKGROUND`), write the results by natural keys and the next stage in one transaction. A finished stage is skipped on redelivery; ≤ 3 attempts per stage on every path with back-off; a never-retry code ends the stage — `consent_required` (nfr-14: a Russian person's audio or text without cross-border consent) among them, and the page then says where the consent is given. Critical stages fail the run (FAILED); the others leave it PARTIALLY_COMPLETED. A recover sweep re-drives stalled and failed-for-retry runs from the DB.

   | Source | Stages |
   |---|---|
   | `live` | findings → write-up (+ map) → [index] → [recollections] → [tasks] |
   | `import`, `recording` | intake → transcribe → diarize (non-critical) → align → findings → write-up (`recording`: per MT-D3) → … |
   | `regenerate` | write-up |

5. **Recordings.** Intake: the object (claimed from files, or assembled from native parts, in `transient/recording/…`) is fetched to the scratch directory; `ffprobe` names the container and codec (anything outside the allowed list is refused before decoding) and gives the duration; an import over 6 hours is refused (`recording_too_long`), and so is a recording whose minutes do not fit — an import's whole length, a desktop recording's length beyond the live meeting's (`MinutesAllowance.covers`, FAILED `upload_exceeds_minutes`, MT-D3); `ffmpeg` cuts parts of ≤ 5 min near silence. Both tools run with a time and a memory limit and no network (MT-D10). Transcribe: parts in parallel (bounded) with timestamps; the raw answer kept as O4. Diarize: pyannote over the whole file; turns kept as O5. Align: lines shaped (§1.2), speakers numbered by first appearance, the owner found by overlap with live "me" lines, the new lines (fresh `seq`s above the meeting's highest) replace the live lines in the span in one transaction. Objects are deleted after the run ends; the bucket rule is the backstop.
   A desktop recording's run rewrites only Leo's automatic write-up (MT-D3); the person's own notes are never written by a run.
6. **Findings and write-up.** Findings: one structured call over the transcript when it fits the model's budget, else windows with overlap and one merging pass; each finding carries its evidence (offset and a short quote) and owner; the map is projected from them. Write-up: the template's sections (shipped prompts in English, or the person's titles) composed from the findings and the transcript, in the meeting's language (MT-D9). Then `notes_revision` increases and `notes.meeting.notes_ready` is written. Regenerate (`POST /v1/notes/{id}/write-up {template_id}`, 10 per minute) runs the write-up only.
7. **Notes sync.** Each person's own notes on a meeting (MT-D12): `PUT …/my-notes` (cabinet) and the native `PUT …/note/draft` write the current user's row, last write wins, `version` + 1, the client recorded; `GET …/my-notes` and the native `GET …/note` read only it, empty before the person writes any. Whoever may read the meeting — the owner, a workspace's member — has their own, and nobody reads another's.
8. **Share link.** `PUT` creates or updates the one link (the token kept across changes), `DELETE` removes it with its comments; the public read redacts per the settings (no emails ever; names hidden → "Speaker n" by first appearance, worded in the reader's language); one refusal for every failure; a hidden or not-kept meeting, a hidden owner or an expired link do not resolve.
9. **Delete** (MT-D8): hidden at once (share link off, workspace shares withdrawn through `notes.meeting.deleted`); purge after 7 days: rows, transient objects by pointer, the tombstone (`UsageTombstones.meeting_deleted`) in the purge's transaction. Support lists and restores a person's hidden meetings, never while the account is in its deletion window, each action in the admin's audit log.
10. **Account data.** Export: meetings with transcript, notes, findings, participants, comments as JSON; erase and transfer through the registry; a hidden account's meetings and links are served to nobody (identity's window).
