# studio — slice plan

Prompt: [prompts/S-feature-slice.md](../prompts/S-feature-slice.md), launched through [BACKEND-next-step.md](../prompts/BACKEND-next-step.md). Status: [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md), row `studio`. Slice card: [SLICE_MAP.md §4.2.19](../SLICE_MAP.md#19-studio). Design, normative: [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../design/ARCHITECTURE.md) §6 (platform data in the EU home), §7, §10, §14 and the [Leo architecture](../../design/leo/ARCHITECTURE.md) §3.3, §7.3, §7.4, §8.5. Cabinet features: `studio` (the Studio screen), `admin-ai-models`, `admin-platform-prompts`, and the model row of Settings › Leo (`settings/ui/leo`), SLICE_MAP §4.3.

`studio` is how Shosai's AI behaves: which models people may choose and what each role runs on by default, the platform's own instructions, the person's prompts per role and the few words about them ("About you") that every answer to them takes into account. It is a blade the person sets once and forgets: nothing in it asks for attention, and everything in it reaches Leo, the desktop answers and the meeting notes without the person repeating themselves.

- **Status:** written on 2026-10-27; **approved by the owner on 2026-10-27** ([ADR 0074](../../adr/0074-studio-slice.md)): ST-D1, ST-D2, ST-D3, ST-D5 and ST-D6 as written, and the points settled without a decision; ST-D4 with the owner's amendment (a tier change waits for the admin's confirmation, nothing already counted is recounted); ST-D7 on the condition that the growth of input tokens and of a turn's cost is measured and L-N3 holds — measured in §1.4. The documents of §5 were amended with the approval.
- **Dependencies:** identity and F6 are `done`; studio's card needs nothing else to start. What it uses from slices in progress is built: billing's `ENTITLEMENTS` and `TOKEN_ALLOWANCE` (billing steps 1–7), agent's skills store and `SkillReader` (agent step 15), the native model proxy (agent step 14), notes' findings and write-up stages (notes step 6), Leo's voice (LEO-voice). Studio closes agent step 18 and notes step 25 (steps 11 and 12 here). `capture`'s in-meeting answers are `todo`; they reach the person's prompts through agent's answer port, which step 11 wires.
- **Philosophy check:** the person tells Shosai about themselves once, in one place, and never again; a prompt and a model per role are optional and quiet — an empty Studio gives good answers. The operator curates a short list of models and a short platform layer instead of tending hundreds of rows and knobs; every automatic change (a model OpenRouter dropped) is reported, never silent.

### 1.2 The draft, for lessons

| Draft behaviour or lesson | What studio does |
|---|---|
| Eight copies of the role list drifted; a role missing from one made autosave fail silently | One `ModelRole` and one `PromptRole` enum in studio's contract; every consumer and the cabinet's generated types read them |
| The active prompt was "the newest active one by `created_at`"; duplicated and Leo-made prompts never took effect | The active prompt is an explicit row per (person, role), with a composite foreign key that keeps its role equal to the prompt's |
| Platform rows were `user_id IS NULL` in the same table; global search leaked the guardrail text to everyone (`9e29d7c50`); an admin could never have personal prompts | Platform prompts live in their own EU tables; people's prompts in their home with `user_id NOT NULL` and the personal RLS policy; the operator is not a person (ADR 0049) |
| No versions, no draft, no rollback, no audit; edits reached processes by four TTL caches, invalidated before commit and only in one process | Immutable numbered versions, a draft, restore as a new version; one platform snapshot per process with a 60 s TTL and no invalidation messages to get wrong |
| Personal context had no cap at injection (text + every file's extracted text) and was writable by external MCP clients | ≤ 4 000 characters checked at write; no files; Leo writes it only through a confirmed card, never over MCP |

### 1.3 Design point

- **Resolutions.** At 10 000 DAU: Leo ≲ 8 turns/s (one agent worker's measured capacity, agent step 17), desktop answers ≈ 9/s (2 600 meetings, one question per 5 minutes), notes 1.6 runs/s (5 850 in the peak hour). ≲ 20 resolutions/s, ≲ 200/s at 100 K DAU. Each reads the person's rows in one statement inside the caller's open transaction (a primary-key lookup; +45 ms for a Russian person, once), plus one statement for skill bodies when the prompt has skills. Platform data comes from a per-process snapshot: about 30 processes × one EU read a minute = 0.5 reads/s.
- **Storage.** Catalogue ≲ 100 rows; a platform version ≲ 100 KB, a few hundred over years, paged. A person: one context ≤ 4 000 characters, ≤ 100 prompts ≤ 20 000 characters, five assignments — typically a few KB; worst case ≈ 4 MB.
- **Admin reads.** The AI models screen reads F6's month summary over both homes once per open; nothing polls.

### 1.4 The profile core's budget, measured (ST-D7)

The owner accepted ST-D7 on the condition that the growth is measured and L-N3 (≤ 12 K input tokens p50 and ≤ 20 K p95 on a first model round, default tier) holds. Measured on 2026-10-27 through Leo's real turn: the 12 scenarios of agent's deterministic suite, each played by the scripted model three times in one thread (no AI spend), with segment 1 and segment 4 raised to ST-D7's caps and the added text put where step 11 puts it. Tokens are agent's estimate (3 bytes a token), the one L-N3's CI check uses; it over-counts Cyrillic, which real tokenizers pack at about 3–4 characters a token. Cost uses `google/gemini-3.8-flash` (staging's `AGENT_STANDARD_MODEL`) at OpenRouter's prices of the day: $0.825 per M input, $0.0825 per M cached input, $4.125 per M output; the first call of a thread pays the full input price, every later call reads the stable head (segments 1–4 and the tools, ≈ 4 800 tokens at baseline) from the cache.

| Variant | First round p50 | p95 | Head | Turn 1 (cold) | Δ | Turns 2–3 (warm) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 4 869 | 4 950 | 4 801 | $0.00508 | — | $0.00155 | — |
| "About you", 600 characters, English (typical) | 5 070 | 5 150 | 5 001 | $0.00527 | +3.6% | $0.00159 | +2.3% |
| "About you", 4 000 characters, English | 6 203 | 6 283 | 6 134 | $0.00630 | +24% | $0.00178 | +15% |
| "About you", 4 000 characters, Russian | 7 339 | 7 419 | 7 270 | $0.00734 | +44% | $0.00198 | +27% |
| Platform layer at its cap (1 000 tokens), everyone | 5 870 | 5 950 | 5 801 | $0.00600 | +18% | $0.00173 | +11% |
| Worst: the layer + 4 000 Russian characters + pinned memory at its 400 | 8 740 | 8 820 | 8 671 | $0.00862 | +70% | $0.00222 | +43% |

- **L-N3 holds** in every variant: the worst first round is 8 740 tokens p50 and 8 820 p95, against 12 K and 20 K.
- **The growth is bounded by the caps, not by the scenarios:** at most +1 000 tokens in segment 1 and +2 870 in segment 4, +3 870 in all. A first round stays within 12 K p50 while today's p50 is below 8.1 K; the suite's is 4.9 K, with an empty history. Real threads carry history and volatile context the suite does not, so step 11 measures the first round again on the live suite (a real model, within $0.5) and on staging's `agent_runs`, and stops for the owner's decision if p50 exceeds 12 K.
- **Cost:** a typical "About you" adds 2–4% to a turn; the full 4 000 characters 15–44%, paid for by the person who wrote them (their tokens count against their allowance); the platform layer at its cap adds 11–18% to every turn, which is the operator's choice when publishing and is shown with the layer's token count before it is published.

**Settled without a decision** (derived by the agent from the documents; the owner may still overrule):

- **Errors:** ADR 0008's envelope with stable codes and parameters (`context_too_long{max}`, `prompt_name_required`, `prompt_name_too_long{max}`, `prompt_too_long{max}`, `prompt_over_budget{tokens, max}`, `too_many_skills{max}`, `skill_not_installed`, `prompt_limit_reached{max}`, `prompt_not_found`, `prompt_for_other_role`, `unknown_role`, `model_not_on_plan`, `model_cant_do_role{model, role}`, `max_tokens_range{min, max}`; admin: `model_not_on_openrouter`, `model_already_listed`, `model_is_default`, `model_disabled`, `default_not_standard`, `no_tier_proposal`, `platform_prompt_not_english{field}`, `platform_layer_over_budget{tokens, max}`, `nothing_to_publish`, `version_not_found`, `openrouter_unreachable`, `management_key_missing`); the cabinet words them (16 languages for Studio, Russian for the admin), as the skills dialog and admin-users do. The check and `sync_error` return codes, not Russian sentences.
- **Data placement:** platform tables only in the EU home's schema (§6, ADR 0010); people's tables in each home; home rows reference catalogue ids softly.
- **Freshness:** the platform snapshot (catalogue, prices view, defaults, the published layer) is read once a minute per process with single flight, like `PriceBook`; an operator's change reaches every process within 60 s, which the screens say. A person's rows are read on every resolution and never cached (they are personal data of a home, and a change must take effect at once).
- **Limits and defaults:** ≤ 100 prompts per person (409 `prompt_limit_reached`); ≤ 20 skills per prompt; a role's prompt and its skills ≤ 12 000 tokens (refused at save; a skill that grew later is left out in order and counted); `max_tokens` 256…min(model's maximum, 32 768); without a row: temperature 0.7, `max_tokens` 4 096 (8 192 for `notes`), reasoning off. The context is stored trimmed; its limit is counted in code points, so the cabinet's stricter UTF-16 count never meets a refusal.
- **Retries:** `POST /v1/studio/prompts`, `POST …/publish` and `POST …/versions/{id}/restore` take an `Idempotency-Key` (effect-key ledger, §10); `PUT` and `PATCH` are idempotent; adding a listed model is 409.
- **Concurrency:** one operator (ADR 0049): the draft is last-write-wins; publish and restore number versions under a row lock on the draft.
- **Versions:** `GET /v1/admin/platform-prompts` returns the draft and the published version; versions are a cursor collection of summaries, and one version is read whole by id when the compare dialog picks it (§10 collections rule).
- **Forecast:** F6's month forecast becomes the screen's rule — month to date plus the mean of the last 7 full business days times the days left; one forecast for the screen and for Leo in the admin panel (AG-D5).
- **Spend outside the catalogue** (transcription, OCR, Jev, embeddings) is returned as its own total, so the top-models shares and the request count add up.
- **Events:** `studio.model.disabled {model_id, reason}` and `studio.model.tier_proposed {model_id, confirmed, proposed}` (ST-D4 as amended), both with `alerting` as their subscriber; `studio.prompts_published` is not emitted until a consumer needs it (event-driven only where justified) — SLICE_MAP's card amended.
- **Built-in role prompts** — the text a role runs on when the person has none, the output contracts and the language directive — stay with the consumer that parses the output (agent for the desktop answers, notes for its stages); studio returns the person's body or none.
- **Skills detached on uninstall:** agent adds `agent.skill.removed {user_id, skill_id}` to its uninstall transaction; studio deletes the skill from the person's prompts in its subscriber (no import cycle).

### 3.3 Resolution of a model

1. **The choice:** the person's assignment for the role, or `leo_model` for `leo` (`auto` is no choice).
2. **Checked by one rule:** listed and not retired, enabled, capable of the role and of the call's `needs`, and frontier only with `frontier_models` on the plan. The tier is always the confirmed one; a pending proposal changes nothing until the admin confirms it (ST-D4). A choice that fails becomes the default, with the reason in `substitution` (counted; agent may show it).
3. **The default:** the role's default and its optional fallback (ST-D3); no default → `model_role_unconfigured`, fail closed, a ticket.
4. **Frontier:** billing's allowance is asked for a frontier primary; "fall back" answers with the role's default and `substitution = frontier_allowance_used` (BL-D4's note under Leo's answer); a standard primary is asked for standard tokens, and "stop" is `token_limit`, as agent does today.
5. **The route:** a standard primary falls back to the default and then to the fallback (same tier, without repeats); a frontier primary has no fallbacks and `standard` = the default, which the kill switch takes. `call_class` is `expensive` for frontier, `standard` otherwise; temperature only when the model supports it, reasoning off when it does not.

Property tests (Hypothesis) prove: a resolved route never names a disabled, retired, incapable or off-plan model; a frontier route never falls back across tiers; the defaults' invariants hold after any sequence of admin writes.

### 3.4 Composition of a prompt

| Consumer | Order |
|---|---|
| Leo, text (Leo §7.3) | segment 1: agent's policy core + the platform layer; segment 3: the active skill playbook (agent's activation); segment 4: the profile core + memory + the personal context; then summary, recent, volatile, question |
| Leo, voice | the voice instruction (capture) + the platform layer + `voice_mode`'s addendum + the personal context |
| Desktop answers (proxy, in-meeting answers through agent) | system: the role's frame (agent: language directive, output contract) + the platform layer → system: the personal context → system: the person's body or the built-in one, then the skills wrapped `skill:user` → the client's messages |
| Notes | notes' findings and write-up prompts + `meeting_summary`'s addendum + the person's `notes` body as a style block; the output contract last; no personal context |

The person's own prompt and context are their own words (taint `none`, Leo §7.4); skills are third-party text, always wrapped and subordinate to policy. The platform layer is the operator's English text, checked at publish.

### 3.6 Data

**EU home only (platform, ADR 0005):**

- `models_registry`: `id` (URL-safe slug, PK), `openrouter_id` (unique), `name`, `maker`, `context_length`, `max_output_tokens`, `capabilities` (`text`, `vision`), `supports_temperature`, `supports_reasoning`, `tier` (confirmed, CHECK standard or frontier), `tier_proposed` (nullable, ≠ `tier`), `tier_proposed_at`, `tier_confirmed_at`, `enabled`, `retired_at`, `upstream_missing_since`, `metadata_synced_at`, `added_at`, `updated_at`. A declined proposal is not raised again for the same price.
- `model_defaults`: `role` (PK, CHECK in `ModelRole`), `model_id` → `models_registry`, `fallback_model_id` → `models_registry` (nullable, ≠ `model_id`), `updated_at`.
- `platform_prompt_drafts`: one row (`id = 1` by CHECK), `config` (JSONB with its `schema` number), `updated_at`.
- `platform_prompt_versions`: `id` (UUIDv7), `number` (unique), `config`, `comment` (≤ 280), `restored_from`, `published_at`; the application role has no UPDATE or DELETE on it.

**Each home (personal, RLS `user_id = app_user_id()`, no foreign key to `users`, erased through the account registry):**

- `user_contexts`: `user_id` (PK), `content` (CHECK ≤ 4 000 code points), `created_at`, `updated_at`.
- `prompt_templates`: `id` (UUIDv7), `user_id`, `name` (1…80), `role` (CHECK in `PromptRole`), `content` (≤ 20 000), `created_at`, `updated_at`; UNIQUE (`id`, `role`); index (`user_id`, `updated_at` DESC).
- `prompt_skills`: (`prompt_id` → `prompt_templates` ON DELETE CASCADE, `skill_id`) PK, `position`; ≤ 20 per prompt.
- `role_prompts`: (`user_id`, `role`) PK, (`prompt_id`, `role`) → `prompt_templates (id, role)` ON DELETE CASCADE — the active prompt's role is the prompt's by construction.
- `model_role_assignments`: (`user_id`, `role`) PK, `model_id` (nullable, a catalogue id), `temperature` (0…2), `max_tokens` (256…32 768), `reasoning`, `updated_at`.

The account data handler exports the five home tables as JSON lines, erases them and copies them on a transfer (the skills' ids move with agent's rows).

### 3.7 Telemetry and monitors

- Metrics: `shosai_studio_route_substitutions_total{role, reason}`, `shosai_studio_resolution_seconds{role}`, `shosai_studio_snapshot_age_seconds`, `shosai_studio_catalogue_refresh_total{outcome}`, `shosai_studio_skills_left_out_total`, `shosai_studio_platform_version`.

- Logs carry ids, roles and codes, never a prompt, a context or a skill body (§14.13).

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The switch of steps 11–12 removes env routes before staging has defaults | The step sets and checks all six defaults on staging before its release; M2's runbook does the same before cutover |
| A long platform layer or a person's long prompt raises the cost of every answer | Budgets checked at publish and save; the layer sits before the cache breakpoint; the person's tokens count against their allowance |
| OpenRouter changes its model list format | The refresh refuses a list it cannot read, keeps the catalogue as it is and opens a ticket; the add shows `model_not_on_openrouter` |
| A price change moves a model to frontier while it counts as standard | The tier changes only on the admin's confirmation (ST-D4 as amended); the proposal is in the monitoring chat at once and a ticket after 3 days; the platform pays the difference meanwhile |
| The trimmed admin screen loses a control the owner wanted | ST-D1 names every removed control and why; an addition later is additive |
